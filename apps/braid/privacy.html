Braid — Privacy Policy

Effective 25 August 2026

Braid is a self-hosted messenger. It is built so that the developer of Braid cannot see your data. There is no Braid cloud, no account with us, and no telemetry. The app talks only to servers you configure.

What Braid collects

Nothing. The app contains no analytics, no crash reporting service, no advertising SDKs, and no tracking of any kind. The developer operates no server that the app talks to.

Where your data goes

Braid communicates exclusively with servers you choose:

  • Your Matrix homeserver. Messages, media, contacts and encryption keys sync with the homeserver you sign in to. That server is operated by you or by whoever you chose; its privacy practices are its operator's.
  • Bridges you install on your own infrastructure relay messages to third-party networks (for example WhatsApp or Telegram). Those networks see what any client on them sees; the bridge runs under your control.
  • Optional AI endpoints. Voice transcription, semantic search embeddings and reply suggestions only run if you configure an endpoint for them. On-device models never send audio or text anywhere. If you point Braid at a remote endpoint, the content you asked it to process is sent to that endpoint — which, again, you chose and control.

What stays on your device

  • Message history, search indexes and media caches, stored locally.
  • Encryption keys, held in the platform keystore or keychain.
  • Contact names, if you enable address-book matching. Matching happens entirely on the device; your address book is never uploaded.
  • Notifications are decrypted on the device.

Permissions

Braid asks for permissions only when a feature needs them: microphone for voice messages and calls, camera for photos and QR device verification, contacts for local name matching, location only when you explicitly share it into a chat, and notifications. Denying a permission disables only the feature that wanted it.

Children

Braid is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Your rights

Your data lives on your homeserver and your device. Deleting the app removes its local data; data on your homeserver is managed through your homeserver. There is nothing held by the developer to request, export, or delete.
